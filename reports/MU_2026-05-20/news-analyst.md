## 1. Executive Summary

Micron sits at the intersection of two powerful but conflicting forces this week. **Bullish:** memory-chip supply is being squeezed by an 18-day Samsung strike threat (worker bonus talks collapsed), reinforcing a structurally undersupplied memory market that peers like Sandisk are pricing in via long-term contracts; multiple sell-side desks (led by Melius) raised price targets — Melius hiked its target to **$1,100**. **Bearish/transient:** semis sold off broadly on May 18 (MU -7.2% intraday) on a combined Samsung-strike risk + TSMC stake sale news that "rattled global chip supply chains," with the entire SOX-style basket (LRCX, KLAC, AMAT, WDC, AMKR, MRVL, MPWR, etc.) trading lower in unison. The market is also holding its breath into **Nvidia earnings (post-close 5/20)**, the de facto AI-cycle catalyst that will determine whether the chip rally extends or unwinds.

The insider tape, however, is unambiguously and persistently **bearish at the corporate-action level**: CEO Sanjay Mehrotra sold **$21.45M** on May 1 and an officer sold **$4.06M** the same day — at sharply higher prices than the prior cohort of Q4 2025 sales. With MU trading in the $780s by mid-May, a Director (Gomo) followed with a $1.57M sale on May 11. Across the past ~12 months, sells outnumber buys roughly **40-to-1 by dollar value**, with **only a single open-market purchase** (Director Liu, $7.8M in January at ~$337) the entire window.

**Net read:** Constructive-to-bullish near-term tape (supply tightness + AI demand pull through Nvidia), but a credibility check from management's own selling behavior at every price step up — including immediately after the AI surge. Position size should respect both the upside catalyst and the insider distribution pattern.

## 8. Bottom-Line Trading Read

The week's news flow is **net bullish** on the structural memory cycle and near-term supply squeeze, with NVDA tonight as a high-conviction catalyst that should rhyme with MU's HBM exposure. The macro backdrop is supportive. **However**, the insider tape is the loudest single counterweight in the dataset: the CEO and multiple officers monetized aggressively into the May print at $500+ levels, and a director sold again at $786 just before this report's date. The risk-reward asks the question — *do you side with the analyst desks raising to $1,100, or with the executives selling at $500–$787?* A balanced read is to respect both: the supply/AI thesis justifies long exposure into NVDA, but the insider behavior argues against full-bore conviction sizing and supports tight risk discipline (e.g., trim/scale on NVDA-day strength, watch for follow-on insider sales after the next earnings window opens).
