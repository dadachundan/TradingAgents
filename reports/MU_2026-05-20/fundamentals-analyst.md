## 1. Company Profile

Micron Technology, Inc. (NASDAQ: MU) is a leading designer and manufacturer of memory and storage semiconductors — DRAM, NAND flash, and increasingly High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) used in AI accelerator stacks. Micron is one of only three commercially significant DRAM vendors globally (alongside Samsung and SK Hynix) and operates in a deeply cyclical industry where supply/demand imbalances drive multi-year boom-and-bust cycles.

**Key observations:**

- **Revenue scaling is parabolic.** Q2 FY26 revenue of $23.86B is **+196% YoY** vs the $8.05B reported a year earlier (2025-02-28) and **+75% sequentially** from $13.64B. Over the five-quarter window revenue has nearly tripled.
- **Gross margin expansion is the dominant story.** GM stepped from 36.8% → 44.7% → 56.0% → **74.4%** in just four sequential quarters. For a commodity-tilted memory company this is extreme; it implies HBM/leading-node DRAM is now an outsized share of mix at near-monopoly economics. Per-bit DRAM ASPs and HBM3E/HBM4 pricing are clearly running well above marginal cost.
- **Operating leverage is enormous.** OpEx grew much slower than revenue: R&D went from $898M → $1,250M (+39%) while revenue grew +196%; SG&A barely budged ($285M → $344M). Operating margin therefore expanded from 22.0% → **67.6%** in five quarters.
- **EPS trajectory:** $1.41 → $1.68 → $2.83 → $4.60 → **$12.07** (diluted). Q2 FY26 alone exceeded the four prior quarters combined.
- **Effective tax rate** ticked up modestly (10.1% → 14.7%) but remains low, suggesting use of U.S. CHIPS Act credits, foreign income mix, and deferred tax assets.
- **Interest income > interest expense** ($155M vs $32M in the latest quarter); interest expense has actually **fallen** as Micron has aggressively paid down debt (see §4).

### TTM vs forward

TTM net income of $24.1B at ~1,142M diluted shares ≈ $21.1 EPS, matching the disclosed TTM EPS of $21.17. Forward EPS of $102.7 implies the Street is modeling another **~5x** EPS expansion as HBM4 ramps and the memory upcycle continues — a very aggressive setup that the current 50DMA $498 share price is already partly discounting (forward P/E ~7).

**Strengths:**

- **Equity has expanded $23.8B in one year** (from $48.6B to $72.5B), driven almost entirely by retained earnings ($43.8B → $66.8B). Book value per share ≈ $64.24.
- **Debt has been actively reduced.** Total debt dropped from a peak of $16.1B (May 2025) to **$10.8B** (Feb 2026) — a ~33% reduction in nine months. Long-term debt ex-leases fell from $12.4B → $7.3B.
- **Liquidity is exceptional.** Cash & short-term investments at $14.6B exceed total debt; net debt is effectively negative if you include the receivables surge. Current ratio 2.90 is comfortable.

**Watch items:**

- **Receivables grew at a startling pace.** Accounts receivable jumped from $5.09B → **$15.39B** in five quarters, with a $7.4B sequential jump in Q2 FY26 alone. Days-sales-outstanding has clearly expanded, which inflates working capital and is a meaningful drag on cash conversion. This is consistent with hyperscaler customers (Nvidia, AMD, AWS, Microsoft, Meta, Google) buying very large lot sizes on extended terms.
- **Inventory is essentially flat** ($9.0B → $8.3B). Given revenue almost tripled, **inventory turns have improved dramatically** — Micron is shipping out faster than it can replenish, consistent with tight DRAM/HBM supply.
- **PP&E is growing fast.** Net PP&E went from $43.2B → **$52.1B**, with Construction-in-Progress up from $4.4B → $7.5B (+69%). Gross PP&E rose $16B in a year. This is the visible fingerprint of HBM capacity additions and the Boise / NY fab build-out.
- **Goodwill is small** ($1.15B, unchanged) — Micron is overwhelmingly an organic-growth story, not M&A-driven.

**Cash flow quality observations:**

## 5. Notable Anomalies and One-Time Items

- **No material restructuring, impairment, or unusual items** flagged in the income statement after 2024-08-31 (which had a tiny -$5M unusual item). The earnings explosion is operationally clean — it is not driven by gains, write-ups, or accounting changes.
- **Other Non-Operating Income/Expense** moved between -$98M and -$140M (small relative to scale); not a swing factor.
- **Sequential discontinuity in margins.** The Q1 FY26 → Q2 FY26 GM jump from 56.0% → 74.4% is unusually large. In memory cycles this kind of margin step typically reflects (a) sudden ASP increases on supply-constrained nodes (HBM3E/HBM4), (b) a favorable product-mix shift, or (c) a true bottleneck where Micron has pricing power. It is unlikely to be sustained at 74.4% across multiple quarters — but even a normalization to ~55-60% would still represent a structural step-up vs prior cycles.
- **Tax rate creep** (10% → 15%) bears watching as utilization of NOLs and CHIPS Act credits unwinds.
- **Concentration / receivables risk.** The $15.4B receivables balance — up 200%+ YoY — implies very large customer concentration (likely a handful of AI hyperscalers). If any major customer slows orders or stretches terms further, the working-capital build could reverse painfully.

## 7. Bottom Line

Micron is operating in what may be the strongest memory upcycle in the company's history, with HBM demand from AI accelerators producing a step-change in revenue, margins, and free cash flow. The Q2 FY26 print (23.9B revenue, 74.4% GM, $12.07 EPS) is extraordinary on every measure. Balance-sheet posture is conservative — management is paying down debt and building cash rather than financial-engineering returns, which is the appropriate playbook given the structural cyclicality of memory.

**Key bull points:**
- Operating leverage proven; HBM mix likely supports above-cycle margins
- Net-cash effective balance sheet, ROE near 40%, forward P/E ~7x
- Capex investment is funding visible HBM/leading-node capacity that should sustain growth

**Key bear / watch points:**
- Receivables explosion ($15.4B, OCF/NI 0.86x) signals earnings quality risk if hyperscaler customers slow or extend terms
- 74.4% gross margin is unlikely to be a steady-state level; the cycle will eventually turn
- 39% capex/revenue intensity means FCF stays well below GAAP earnings during the build phase
- Stock has already re-rated dramatically (50DMA $498 vs 200DMA $313, 52-week low $91) — much of the good news is priced in; future returns hinge on whether forward EPS of $102+ proves achievable

The fundamentals are pristine, but the cyclical setup is mature. A discerning trader should weight (a) the duration of HBM tightness, (b) the receivables-collection cadence, and (c) hyperscaler capex commentary as the primary near-term catalysts.
